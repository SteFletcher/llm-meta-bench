llm-meta-bench · intent comparison · solar-bench

Same prompt, three solar systems.

Ten builds were handed the identical brief — a sprawling spec for an “AAA-quality, browser-based, real-time Solar System simulator” that should feel like a game, not a website. These are the three highest-scoring submitted results: Claude Opus 5, Claude Fable 5 and Kimi K3. Each owned the whole build. To expose how their priorities differ, I used each app's own navigation to fly to Earth and captured the arrival.

Explicit execution · the what Did it deliver the requested product?

Required bodies, science, controls, rendering, accessibility, documentation and verification.

Implicit intent · the why Did it understand the experience?

A credible, cinematic simulation that feels like a premium game and stays compelling on a second monitor.

Why equal weight? A perfect checklist with no believable world has missed the assignment. A beautiful globe with dead controls is not a finished product. Neither failure mode can hide inside the other axis. Full scoring method →

Claude Opus 5

opus5-solar-demo · “SOL”IN $5 · OUT $25 / MTok ↗

Got right

  • The deepest verified build. Typecheck, lint, 58 unit tests and 44 production browser tests pass across the renderer, catalogue, cameras, tours, widgets, accessibility and photo mode.
  • A complete living system. Forty-four bodies, 160,054 stars, 27,000 minor bodies, six tours, procedural audio and hardware WebGPU all operate together.
  • Excellent arrival. Earth fills the frame on the day side with independent clouds, specular water and a thin atmospheric limb.
  • Science and scale are load-bearing. JPL/Standish elements, NASA physical data, a tested Kepler solver, true scale and a floating origin shape the experience.

Fell short

  • Invented geography. The continents are procedural rather than recognisable Earth data; population-shaped night lights are absent.
  • Maximum zoom loses the world. At 127 km altitude the surface becomes a soft green field instead of revealing continent or cloud detail.
  • Hard-body finish is uneven. Saturn is excellent, but Jupiter's Great Red Spot is not visible and the close Sun clips toward white.

Claude Fable 5

sol-simulator · “SOL”IN $10 · OUT $50 / MTok ↗

Got right

  • A believable ocean world. Procedural coast-like landmasses, blue specular oceans, layered drifting clouds and a thin atmospheric limb.
  • Cinematic framing. The fly-to arrives on the day side, and Saturn's rings sweep deliberately across the foreground.
  • Deep product coverage. Full catalogue, tested Kepler propagation, camera modes, search, tours, audio, live widgets and worker-built belts all ship.
  • Strong checks. Production build, lint, 11 unit tests and 21 browser tests pass.

Fell short

  • Invented geography. The attractive continents and city-light density are procedural fiction rather than Earth data.
  • Less implementation depth than Opus. It remains complete, but its catalogue, accessibility work and passing verification stack are smaller.
  • No real surface shortcut. Like Opus and Kimi, it spends engineering effort generating planets rather than borrowing credited public maps.

Kimi K3

kimi3-solar-demo · “S·O·L”IN $3 · OUT $15 / MTok ↗

Got right

  • The most game-first system. Eight thousand worker-built asteroids, two comets, solar wind, rotating clouds and six tours make it an excellent “leave it running” interpretation.
  • Serious engine breadth. Thirty focused modules, full catalogue, gamepad support, true-scale mode, live renderer counters and a deep control surface.
  • Strong arrival. Earth is deliberately day-lit, fills the frame and remains legible inside an unusually dense scene.
  • Core checks hold. Build, lint and 16 unit tests pass; physical data and Kepler propagation are sound.

Fell short

  • Invented surface data. Every planet uses a 512×256 generated canvas, so Earth cannot pass the coastline or deep-zoom probes.
  • Visual excess. The cyan atmospheric shell, orbit graphics, asteroid belt and bright particles compete with the planet.
  • No browser suite. The README claims browser-automation verification, but no E2E suite ships with the build.
The read

Opus wins the simulator. Fable keeps it close. Kimi keeps it alive.

These are genuinely the field's top three, but they win different versions of the brief. Opus 5 is the deepest complete and verified simulator: hardware WebGPU, 44 bodies, true-scale science, strong staging and 102 passing automated checks. Fable 5 is the closest balanced product, joining a leaner engine with excellent interaction and verification. Kimi K3 builds the most game-first living system, with unusually rich motion and machinery around the planets.

The ranking turns on how much survives inspection. All three invent geography, so none delivers the real-Earth money shot. Opus accepts that same visual compromise but proves more of everything around it—catalogue, cameras, accessibility, tours, diagnostics and performance—so it leads at 9.3.
