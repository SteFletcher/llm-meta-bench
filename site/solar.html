Same prompt, three Earths.

Twelve builds were handed the identical brief — a sprawling spec for an “AAA-quality, browser-based, real-time Solar System simulator” that should feel like a game, not a website. This is not the top three; it is a deliberate three. GPT-6 Astra leads the field, and Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Fable 5 are one model generation apart on the same brief. Put side by side they show the money shot moving: an invented planet, then a licensed one, then a sharp one. I used each app's own navigation to reach Earth and captured the arrival exactly as it presents it. Claude Opus 5, third in the field, sits out so the two Fable generations can be read against each other.

Explicit execution · the what Did it deliver the requested product?

Required bodies, science, controls, rendering, accessibility, documentation and verification.

Implicit intent · the why Did it understand the experience?

A credible, cinematic simulation that feels like a premium game and stays compelling on a second monitor.

Why equal weight? A perfect checklist with no believable world has missed the assignment. A beautiful globe with dead controls is not a finished product. Neither failure mode can hide inside the other axis. Full scoring method →

Brief: solar-bench/PROMPT.md Stack asked: Three.js · TypeScript · Vite Target: cinematic · scientifically accurate Task: navigate → Earth → screenshot Selection: field leader + both Fable generations Axes: explicit execution · implicit intent

Capture method. Each capture uses the app's own Earth control, lets the arrival settle, then screenshots the interface as shipped — no photo mode, so the HUD each build chose is part of the comparison. All three are live sessions of my own — GPT-6 and Fable 5.1 on hardware WebGPU on 9 September, Fable 5 on its WebGL2 path on 7 September. All three are dark-UI builds, so the frames are directly comparable. Ranks on each card are positions in the full twelve-build field, which is why they read 01, 02 and 04.

GPT-6 Astra

gpt6-solar-demo · “SOL Observatory”IN $10 · OUT $50 / MTok ↗

Got right

  • The sharpest Earth in the field. An 8K day map streams in on approach, with a clean terminator, polar aurora and the Moon in frame for depth.
  • Telemetry that tells the truth. Its panel reports 73 draw calls and 364,749 triangles live from the renderer — where Fable 5.1, a generation newer than the build beside it, reports one and one.
  • Documented limits. A science document enumerates every approximation, and the ledger marks the billion-star requirement “not met literally” rather than claiming it.
  • The fastest full verification. Build, lint, 36 unit and 22 browser tests pass across four spec files in 3.3 minutes.

Fell short

  • It built an observatory, not a game. The brief asked for a AAA sci-fi interface; this is a three-column editorial layout. Beautiful, and the wrong side of the instruction.
  • Slow travel. Cross-system transits run very long, and the top-level tabs stop responding while a voyage is in progress.
  • Declined two explicit items. No temporal anti-aliasing and no IAU pole orientations — both documented rather than hidden, but both absent.

Claude Fable 5.1

fable51-solar-demo · “SOL”TOKEN PRICING not published

Got right

  • The stack the brief specified. Three.js on WebGPU with an automatic WebGL2 fallback, TSL node materials and temporal anti-aliasing on a velocity buffer.
  • The fullest catalogue up here. Forty-three bodies, 33,000 belt and Kuiper objects and 61,000 instanced stars.
  • Every widget the brief named. Thirteen discrete panels to GPT-6's eight, including the Hill spheres and Lagrange points its own predecessor never attempted at all.
  • A real Earth, properly credited. Licensed 2K day, cloud, night-light and specular maps, with an in-app credits panel built from its own licence registry.

Fell short

  • The sky is nearly empty. Its Milky Way panorama loads and is then lit to invisibility, leaving coarse point-stars on black.
  • Its own HUD is wrong. Visible in this capture: one draw call and one triangle under WebGPU, against the ~50 and ~240k its performance notes claim.
  • Looser concept search. “The red planet” returns Saturn alongside Mars, where both rivals return Mars alone.

Claude Fable 5

fable5-solar-demo · “SOL”IN $10 · OUT $50 / MTok ↗

Got right

  • The best sky anyone produced. A procedural Milky Way and nebulae fill every wide frame — the single thing its own successor gave away, and still unmatched in the field.
  • Nothing to download. Every surface is synthesised in the fragment shader, so the whole build is a 200 KB bundle with no texture budget at all.
  • Diagnostics you can trust. Its statistics panel reports real draw calls and triangles, and its renderer comment states the WebGL2 decision outright rather than implying the newer stack.
  • Fast, complete verification. Build, lint, 11 unit and 21 browser tests pass in 1.9 minutes — the quickest full suite of the three.

Fell short

  • Invented geography. The continents are convincing and they are not Earth's — the gap both newer builds closed by licensing real map data.
  • The requested stack, unbuilt. It detects WebGPU and deliberately stays on WebGL2. Honest, and still not what the brief asked for.
  • Fewer instruments. Seven widget modules, with no Hill spheres, Lagrange points, orbital planes or body comparisons at all.
The read

The planet got real. The sky got worse.

Read left to right these are three answers to the same question, and read right to left they are a history. Fable 5 invented its Earth in a shader and hung it in front of the best sky anyone produced. Fable 5.1, one generation later, licensed real map data and got a recognisable planet — then lit its Milky Way into invisibility. GPT-6 Astra streams an 8K day map and puts the Moon in frame for depth, and reports honest telemetry while doing it.

The trade is the point. Every build that fixed the Earth paid for it somewhere in the sky: Fable 5.1's backdrop fell from 5 to 3.5 in the generation that gained real coastlines, and GPT-6 only claws that back to 4.5. Nobody has yet shipped Fable 5's sky and a real planet. The money shot and the backdrop are still being traded against each other, one generation at a time.
