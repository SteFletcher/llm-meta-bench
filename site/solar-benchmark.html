Twelve systems. One impossible brief.

Solar Bench separates the what from the why. Twelve builds received the same sprawling brief. We score whether each build delivered what it literally requested—and whether it created the experience the brief was trying to describe.

Explicit execution · the what Did it deliver the requested product?

Required bodies, science, controls, rendering, accessibility, documentation and verification.

Implicit intent · the why Did it understand the experience?

A credible, cinematic simulation that feels like a premium game and stays compelling on a second monitor.

Why equal weight? A perfect checklist with no believable world has missed the assignment. A beautiful globe with dead controls is not a finished product. Neither failure mode can hide inside the other axis.

The verdict

The best build is no longer the most compliant one.

Every previous leader here topped the board by delivering more of the checklist. GPT-6 does the opposite: it loses the explicit axis to Fable 5.1, 76.5 to 77.5 — fewer bodies, eight tool panels to thirteen, no IAU pole orientations, no temporal anti-aliasing — and still finishes three tenths ahead, because it wins implicit intent 48 to 45.

It wins that axis on honesty more than on beauty. Its telemetry reports 73 draw calls and 364,749 triangles where Fable 5.1's reports one and one; its ledger marks the billion-star requirement "not met literally" instead of quietly claiming it; its science document lists every approximation it makes. The irony is that the most trustworthy build in the field is also the one that ignored the brief's clearest instruction — it was asked for a AAA game interface and built an editorial observatory.

Pick GPT-6 Astra for anything you must trustWorking telemetry, a stated validity window, a published list of what it does not do, and the fastest full suite in the field.
Pick Fable 5.1 for the requested productThe specified rendering stack, 43 bodies, thirteen widget panels and the game interface the brief actually described.
Pick Fable 5 for the frame you leave upIts procedural Milky Way and Sun remain the best-looking idle screen anyone produced.
04 · Scoring method

How the two-axis score works

Every criterion is scored from 0 (absent) to 5 (fully delivered and verified). Optional audio and extras are recorded but excluded from the headline result.

Explicit execution · A

Sixteen required criteria

Audits the literal brief: rendering stack, architecture, catalogue completeness, science, camera, zoom, visuals, UI, controls, accessibility, performance engineering, tests and deliverables.

The question: did the model build what was requested?

Implicit intent · B

Ten experience probes

Tests the meaning behind the list: resourcefulness, a believable Earth, cinematic arrivals, game feel, honesty, substance, scientific soul, idle life, scale and hard-body detail.

The question: did the model understand why it was requested?

Equal-weight result

Neither axis can dominate

Each subtotal is normalized to 10, then the two axes are averaged. This makes experience quality as consequential as checklist coverage.

Explicit = A ÷ 8
Implicit = B ÷ 5
Overall = (Explicit + Implicit) ÷ 2

What actually ran

A missing test suite is not a pass. A broken lint command is not “configured tooling.”

Evidence caveat. GPT-5.6 Sol was restored to its sole commit before recapture; its later uncommitted photographic-texture upgrade is excluded. The in-app browser exposed no backend, so reproducible project-owned Chromium harnesses drove standardized captures. Sonnet's isolated production rerun passed 7/29 E2E cases. SwiftShader FPS was not used to rank visual quality; Opus 5's separately identified measurement used hardware-backed WebGPU. Fable 5.1 was assessed on 7 Sep 2026 from a live hardware-WebGPU browser session driven alongside Fable 5, plus a clean-room command-line run of its lint, typecheck, build, unit and E2E suites; its frame rate is not scored, because its own draw-call counter is wrong under WebGPU and Fable 5's render loop stalls when unfocused. GPT-6 Astra was assessed on 9 Sep 2026 from a live hardware-WebGPU session alongside Fable 5.1 plus a clean command-line run of its lint, build, unit and E2E suites; its Saturn frame is the project's own hardware capture, because throttled requestAnimationFrame made its cross-system transits impractical to drive in that session.
